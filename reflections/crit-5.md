# Crit 5 — A game

**The breakthrough.**

I spent most of Saturday balancing a game against a player who does not exist.

The rules are a pure module, so I could run two minutes of play in a few
milliseconds and ask the computer which upgrades were worth taking. That part
worked, and it told me three of my seven cards were doing nothing at all —
which mattered because the cards have no words on them, so nobody could have
worked that out by reading. I would never have found it by playing.

Then I opened the page and stood still for six seconds and killed nothing. Not
one enemy. Every headless run I had done was full of kills, because the
simulated player is always moving, and a moving player drags its weapon across
things. Standing still was a case my measurement could not produce and a person
finds in the first second.

So the breakthrough wasn't the simulation. It was noticing that the simulation
and my hands catch different bugs, and that neither one is the backup for the
other. I ended up going back and forth: measure, play, measure again.

**What it changed.**

I used to think of automated checks as the rigorous thing and playing as the
soft thing you do afterwards to feel good. This week they were two instruments
pointed at different faults. My bot won fifteen runs out of fifteen no matter
what it picked, and it was that — not a crash, not a red test — that told me
the measurement had stopped measuring anything. I want to keep asking what a
green result is actually made of.
